Headwind, tailwind or crosswind: rowing course wind explained

A short explanation of rowing course wind and how Rowing Weather displays live conditions, course wind, forecasts and saved waters.

Wind direction matters as much as wind speed

The same wind can be a headwind in one direction, a tailwind after turning around and a crosswind on another leg of a course. Reading only a general wind-speed forecast can hide those differences. A course-relative view helps coaches and rowers anticipate which sections may feel different.

Use the course wind analyser

Rowing Weather lets you mark a course and view headwind, tailwind and crosswind components along the selected direction. Combine that with live conditions, gusts, rain information and hourly or seven-day forecasts when planning. Forecasts can change, and displayed conditions are estimates, not on-water measurements.

Plan with local judgement

Wind and weather tools are aids to preparation, not a substitute for your club's safety procedures, official warnings or observations at the slipway. Check current conditions before launching, and adjust or cancel a session when the weather or water is unsuitable.
